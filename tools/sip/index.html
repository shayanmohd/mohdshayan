How is the SIP maturity value calculated?

Each instalment is invested at the start of its month and compounds monthly, so FV = P × ((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i × (1 + i), where P is the monthly amount, i is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. ₹5,000 a month at 12% for 10 years has i = 0.01 and n = 120, which gives about ₹11,61,695 from ₹6,00,000 invested.

What does the annual step-up do?

It raises the monthly amount by a fixed percentage after every 12 months, the way a step-up SIP keeps pace with a rising salary. With a 10% step-up, ₹5,000 a month becomes ₹5,500 in the second year, ₹6,050 in the third and about ₹11,790 in the tenth. At 12% over 10 years that grows to about ₹16,87,163 from ₹9,56,245 invested, against ₹11,61,695 without the step-up.

How much should I invest each month to reach a goal?

Goal mode turns the maturity formula round: P = FV × i ÷ (((1 + i)n − 1) × (1 + i)). To reach ₹1 crore in 20 years at 12%, i = 0.01 and n = 240, so P is about ₹10,009 a month. The year-by-year table then shows how that SIP builds up to the target.

Is a lump sum compounded monthly or yearly?

You choose, and the formula panel states which. Yearly, the default, uses FV = A × (1 + r)years, the way fund returns (CAGR) are quoted: ₹1,00,000 at 12% for 10 years becomes ₹3,10,585. Monthly uses FV = A × (1 + r ÷ 12)months and gives ₹3,30,039 for the same figures, because each month's growth starts earning growth of its own.

What does the value in today's money mean?

Enter an inflation rate and the maturity value is also shown divided by (1 + inflation)years, which is what that sum would buy today. ₹11,61,695 after 10 years of 6% inflation is worth about ₹6,48,685 in today's money. The return you enter is an assumption, not a promise: market returns vary from year to year, and fund expenses, exit loads and tax are not included.
